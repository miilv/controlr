## 1. The flat-long cut replaced by a bound from the gripper

Round 3's `REACH_FLAT = 0.28` (thinnest / longest box side) was a cut placed between the dev catalogue's phone (0.20)
and the rest (≥ 0.34): a category key in disguise. It is gone.

**Rule now (branch `ab0d2673`).** A far-side object goes by table handover first when a centred grasp cannot close
stably across its box. That holds when both of these are true:

- **it is longer than the widest opening**, `2 × FINGER_MAX` = 2 × 0.044 = **0.088 m**. FINGER_MAX is the URDF
  `joint7/8` upper limit in `kinematics.py`. Only the box's narrow side then fits between the pads;
- **its side faces stand lower than one fingertip pad**, `PAD_LENGTH` = 0.1576 − 0.128 = **0.0296 m**. That is the
  pad's extent along the approach in the X5 finger mesh (link7/8, flange x 0.128–0.1576), the same zones
  `X5Kin.finger_points` already uses. The pads then have less than one pad length of contact. The object's height is
  its perceived world box (`top_z − bottom_z`).

Every number comes from the robot. Nothing comes from the catalogue or a task. The other handover trigger stays as
it was: this object's own failed direct pick (a slip or an air close) by the container arm.

**G3 gate (17 dev demo layouts, same bundle):**

| Arm | mean | successes | steps median / max | per-layout vs current B |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| current B (main `2e7690e8`, `("phone",)` key) | 100.0 | 17 / 17 | 1324 / 1573 | — |
| **robot-derived bound (`ab0d2673`)** | **100.0** | **17 / 17** | 1324 / 1573 | **equal score and equal steps on all 17** |

The bound decides the same way as the old cut on every object in these layouts. That is a consequence of the
geometry: the phone is 0.115 m long with 0.022 m sides; the long toy cars are 0.106–0.109 m long but their sides are
0.037–0.039 m tall, higher than a pad; every other mover is shorter than 0.088 m. The bound was not tuned to make
that happen. The legality test and the converged scan: zero new findings.

## 2. Public-timing demos: implemented, measured, and they do NOT come out short

**Implemented.** `scripts/simlab/demo_gen_public.py` (branch `4aa6cac9`) is a dev entry script, default-off, that
never writes a registered block. It uses `demo_gen`'s planning, waypoints, validation and index, with only the timing
replaced by RoboDojo's public procedure:
- the planner's own time-parametrised interpolation (`CuroboPlanner.plan_path` / `plan_joint` →
  `get_interpolated_plan()`, `interpolation_dt` = the sim dt 0.004);
- no pace caps, no 40-sub-step floor;
- gripper moves of 100 sub-steps (90 % interpolation);
- no extra settle or hold.

It is a script, not a `demo_gen --timing` flag, because `dojo/simlab/demo_gen.py` sits in the legality-scanned call
graph and every new line there was a new unresolved finding (the full suite's frozen-baseline test failed). The
script sits outside the scanned roots; `demo_gen` keeps only retired comments.

**Retired:** the 25 Oct (`41a8c94a`) comments quoting the pace of RoboDojo's own make_kong / tic-tac-toe demos
("median 0.002, peak 0.008 rad / sub-step", "RoboDojo-paced demos ~575", "free moves stay at RoboDojo's own peak").
Those numbers came from official `Traj` files, a forbidden source. They are gone from the code and from `--speed`'s
help. `demo_gen`'s legacy pace constants stay at their current values only so the registered blocks reproduce; the
public script uses none of them.

**Measured** on compute3, physics-only, on scratch copies of the layouts (never a registered block):

| Layouts | our legacy demos (env steps) | **public timing, measured** | round 3's estimate (time-optimal at the joint limits) | valid within the 900-step cap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| canonical dev 100005–09, 100024–29 (11) | 630 / 752 / 896 | **861 / 931 / 1009** | 440 / 493 / 546 | 4 (861–897); 7 over 900 |
| IMIT2 fresh salt (19) | 570 / 795 / 900 | **857 / 897 / 1041** | 412 / 473 / 520 | 9 (857–897); 9 over 900; 1 failed validation (100021) |

(min / median / max.) The demos that pass are valid by today's check: RoboDojo's post-demo check and no t-object
moved.

**Why the estimate was wrong.** cuRobo's interpolated plans are not minimum-time. Arm-move durations come out in steps
of 20 sub-steps, with a 101-sub-step floor. Half of all arm moves last ≤ 201 sub-steps (0.8 s), the most common
being exactly 201, even for a 5 cm vertical grasp descent. Per segment on fresh 100000 (sub-steps), public vs legacy:
- grasp descents 1285 vs 808; lowers 905 vs 313; clears 905 vs 202 (the short vertical moves get longer);
- free moves 1225 vs 2152; grips 500 + 500 vs 900 + 350; home 301 vs 667 (the long moves and the grips get shorter).

The two roughly cancel.

**What this means.** RoboDojo's public planner timing does not give ≤ 640-step demos for our waypoints. Whatever
generated the official demos (if they really are ≤ 640) is not the public planner used as-is, or uses fewer or
different waypoints; that procedure is not public. **This is the written limit:** from public code alone I cannot
reproduce the official timing.

Options for the lead (none implemented):
- (a) keep the legacy pace, with its provenance comments retired;
- (b) a time-optimal re-timing at the Franka's public joint limits. That was round 3's estimate, ~410–550 steps,
  physically grounded but not "RoboDojo's procedure"; it needs validation runs, and fast descents failed validation
  before;
- (c) fewer waypoints (merge pre-grasp and grasp, lift and carry).

The 100001 reset stall is not addressed by (a) as-is.

## 3. Proposal: label-agnostic GT matching in the #417 identity instrument (not changed)

**Evidence (round 3's 133 B / C writes).**
- All 133 resolved to the GT object with the **same name** as the belief label (`gt_label == label` for 133 / 133).
  Four of them came from a consumed pick; 129 came from the nearest fallback.
- A one-to-one spatial match of each episode's frame-0 belief record to the layout (10 cm cap) puts **124 / 133**
  written labels on a **different** GT object than the same-named one.
- Median error before a write is 461 mm, about the distance between unrelated objects.

On imitate, perception names objects `t0..t4` / `aim0..aim4` in its own order. The layout uses the same names for
other objects. The instrument therefore measured each write against the wrong object 93 % of the time.

**Proposal.** In `dojo.perception.audit_identity.AuditIdentity`:
1. Never accept label-name equality as identity evidence. A belief label and a GT label are matched only by geometry
   or by action evidence.
2. Seed identity with a one-to-one spatial assignment (Hungarian, capped, say at 50 mm) of the frame-0 belief record
   to GT at step 0, before any write.
3. Keep the consumed-pick / grasp-close finalisation as it is; it overrides the seed.
4. Make the nearest fallback label-agnostic (nearest GT body centre among all instances), and report per run how
   many writes the seed, a pick, or a fallback resolved.
5. Re-score the existing registered audits with the revised instrument as a supplementary table only. A registered
   verdict stays as registered.

Tests: a planted episode where perception's `t1` sits on GT `t4` must be scored against `t4`; the KONG / peg re-scores
must reproduce where the labels were already consistent.
