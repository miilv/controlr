## Public checker and instruction contract

`task/RoboDojo/tasks/pour_by_language.py` allows 800 steps. The instruction
names three ordered pairs by bottle and bowl colors, with first/second/third
ordinals. It specifies neither a selectable liquid nor an amount. The palette
comes from public category indices: red/turquoise/violet bottles and
black/white/brown bowls. Grounding uses instruction clauses and object colors,
not checker labels. Internal belief handles are translated into instance names.

Each bottle must cross back into upright (axis within 30 degrees) after its
fluid predicate passes. The predicate requires at least 95% capture of poured
liquid, at most 10% residual, zero box buffers and 1 mm target-bottom clearance.
It can ignore disconnected outside components smaller than 12 particles at
6 mm XY connectivity, up to 20% of the original particles. This is **not** 95%
of the original particle count: the denominator excludes ignored and residual
particles. The single-cup reference allows 400 steps, requires 97% capture and
at most 15% residual; connectivity is 6.5 mm and component size 7.

Language has home-triggered ordered score tiers 20/50/100. The nested home
condition ORs the arms: an idle arm staying home prevents another rising edge.
Consequently zero intermediate credit cannot diagnose fluid failure. We do not
move an idle arm to exploit this trigger; full successes are the outcome.
G3 candidate diagnostics separately report pending upright checks, the final
public fluid predicate and raw target/residual/outside counts. The predicates
are observed after the episode and never supplied to a policy.

## Original port audit and exploratory mechanisms

Read-only shallow original: `msannikov03/robot-harness`, head
`6769a6c5b3df8c55f56b0324cfdf196084b9ec94`. No sealed measurements were read.

- Our vendored `pour_bottle` matches its v1 file apart from attribution. Grasp
  fraction .72, 0.10 m horizontal approach, lift clearance, staged tilt
  0/40/70/90/105/120/135/150, drain 24, and untilt 90/60/0 are identical.
- Our adapter retains its 0.1501 m flange-to-grasp offset, 0.05/0.08 rad per
  step holding/empty limits, slow multiplier .6, ramp 2 and gripper ramp .25.
  Numerical IK replaces cuRobo, and catalogue mesh clouds replace live USD
  mesh clouds. Baseline tracking evidence does not establish IK as the cause.
- The original strict RGB bottle wrapper actually runs **v5**, not the v1
  implementation we ported. V5 uses mandatory raised waypoint retries and
  checked put-downs; its ordered composite adds adaptive drain budgets,
  pre-flow-only retries, stop-after-failure and checked held-bottle recovery.
  Generic finish must not open a bottle that this composite still holds.
- The strict wrapper supplies synthetic bottle geometry without mesh grasps,
  so its nominal mouth radius is 15 mm. Our baseline's mesh-derived estimate
  is 6 mm. This difference is a hypothesis, not a proven explanation of the
  aggregate 5/5 versus 0/5 D-C19 result.
- Original v2 has sparse angles and target-row waypoints; v5 explicitly reverted
  sparse defaults for single pours after regressions. Both mechanisms therefore
  need physical screening, not adoption based on source comparison.

Default-off `ported_pour_profile` is mirrored in RuntimeConfig, Skills and G3:
`off` preserves dispatch, `sparse` uses v2 without waypoints, `via` uses v2 with
waypoints, and `checked` uses v5 (the original composite for ordered pours).
Existing `pour_order` remains default-off and rejects incomplete/repeated-color
assignments. The original files carry provenance; vendored v1 is untouched.

Pilot records: fresh 100070–72, salt `pour-pilots-20261107-bc6a21`, all stable.
Five exploratory arms, each with aim b10: v1 aim-only, sparse, via, checked,
and existing overlap. None is a registered result. Pilot results pending.
