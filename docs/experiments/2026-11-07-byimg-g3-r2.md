## Controlled phase diagnosis

On the same ten isolation draws, add only +205 mm horizontal belt phase to
the riders before reset. With the downstream arm occupied by the stopped
basket, target pickup falls **9/10→6/10** at 125 mm and **8/10→6/10** at
150 mm (0 phase wins / 3 and 2 losses respectively). Both shifted profiles
have **four no-intercept endings**; the native-phase 125 mm profile has none.
Median target x after stopping shifts from **−0.780 m to −0.337 m**. The
matched per-layout median advance is **397 mm**: the initial 205 mm phase plus
another **192 mm** (about 48 belt steps) while stop/drag takes longer. Failed shifted searches reject 4–48 candidates
against the basket, plus table/IK rejects. Arm choice alone did not improve
the free native-phase 125 mm picks (both arms and left-only each 6/10).

This separates the round-1 phase artifact and occupied-arm timing from simple
lift-height selection. It does not attribute every historical failure to
phase or make the new lift-first target picks universally reliable.

## Additional negative approaches

The centered high drop alone found no reachable release on the noodles pilot.
Lowering the basket in air admits release poses, but noodles leave the fingers
during the carry. SLOW instead of CARRY does not repair that grasp. Scoping the
basket rim family off for the target fails to pick the same noodles (0–3 mm
rise). Rise-before-crossing waypoints either fail native IK, find no subsequent
central release, or still lose the target; this route is excluded from the
frozen candidate/retest. The 24-profile end-wall matrix, in contrast, sustains
>10 cm bottom lift on both pilots at shallow approach angles; 40/55-degree
approaches often cannot plan. A valid cooked representation contains 64 hulls;
the highest isolated bar hull spans about 9.7×45.6×12.6 mm, consistent with a
thin grasp surface rather than an assumed closed solid arch.
