## 1. insert_key: what the checker scores

`task/RoboDojo/tasks/insert_key.py` scores a transition ladder of two rungs:
- 15: the key lifted 5 cm.
- 100: all at once:
  - the key's bbox bottom more than 25 mm under the lock's bbox top;
  - its root within 7 mm (xy) of the lock's;
  - its z axis within 7 deg of up;
  - its x axis within 30 deg of the lock's x turned -60 deg (signed: turned -30 to -90 deg).

The 100 rung is also the success test, so the rung is all or nothing. An insert without the turn scores 15, the same as a
lift. There is no gripper condition: the key may stay in the hand, and the episode ends on success.

## 2. insert_key: a handover that holds

**Why five versions of the in-air handover lost the key.**
- `plan_air_handover` holds the object in its FINAL orientation at the handover. The giver turned the lying key upright.
- The giver held it across its round bow. That is a hinge about the closing axis, and the turn was about that same
  axis, so the key pivoted in the pads and fell.

**The key's geometry** (mesh; colliders: a bow cylinder, a shaft box, two tooth boxes):
- a 20 mm round bow, 10 mm thick, around the origin;
- a 5 x 5 mm shaft down to z -52.8 mm;
- two teeth on +x, z -48.9..-45.5 and -38.1..-34.7 mm, out to x 12.5-12.7 mm;
- centre of mass at the bow / shaft junction (z -11 mm);
- lying, its +y face is up and its bow points at the robot.

Inserted, the bow's faces are perpendicular to the lock's y, so a grasp from the lying key's top face would insert with
the hand pointing back at the robot.

**Giver grasps (probes, layout 100000, right arm):**

| giver grasp | lift (in-hand change) | carry to the handover |
|---|---|---|
| across the 5 mm shaft, below the bow | 5.1 mm / 11 deg | +16 mm / 37 deg |
| lengthwise, pads on the bow's end and the tip, straight down, fingertips 2.8 mm over the table | 0.1 mm / 0.1 deg | +0.1 mm / 0.0 deg (32 steps, eased line at 0.04 rad/step) |

- Gravity has no lever about a lengthwise closing axis (it runs through the centre of mass).
- Both pads press flat ends.
- A tilted straight-down grasp (the approach along -y_key, 3 deg off vertical) dipped its open fingertips into the
  table margin, so the grasp is exactly straight down.

**Taker grasp: chosen at the target, used at the handover.**
- The grasp is across the bow's faces (closing along y_key). The approach lies in the key's x-z plane and is tilted
  15-60 deg down at the target.
- A horizontal hand over the lock block hits it: the bow sits 23 mm over the block's top (place plan "descend:
  collision: gripper hits slot").
- At the handover, with the key still lying, the same grasp comes in sideways from the bow's rim; its fingers clear the
  giver's end pad by >= 3 mm.
- Probe v5: the taker closed and the giver let go; the key stayed 0.6 mm from where it closed. Then the insertion:
  tip 28.8 mm under the lock's top, root 1.4 mm off, upright within 0.2 deg, 233 of 300 steps.
- Only the turn failed: -17.7 deg against the needed -30.

## 3. insert_key: the turn

Probes `key_t1..t5`: the key spawned standing in its lock (the fit pose, tip on the floor), gripped as after the
handover, turned 3 deg per env step.
- **Free to -14 deg, then caught.** In the lock's mesh the rotation cavity (two layers, z 27.9-33.6 and 38.7-44.4 mm,
  for the two teeth) is narrower at r 12.5-12.9 mm between -15 and -36 deg than the teeth's outer corners
  (r 12.7-12.9).
- **Forced on.** Past the catch the key tilts 7-11 deg in the hand. Its root slides up to 6 mm sideways. At -30 to
  -36 deg it snaps through with 5-7 deg of tilt for a step or two, and then the checker can pass.
- **Height matters, the shift does not.**
  - Turned 0.3 / 0.9 / 1.2 mm above the seat: success at that step (episode ended).
  - 0.6 mm: missed by 0.3 deg of tilt (7.3 deg at -34 deg of yaw).
  - Pressed 1-2 mm onto the floor: never.
  - Shifting the root 0.6-1.8 mm away from the teeth while turning did not help.
- **What the skill does** (`regrasp.turn_held`): insert holding on (`release=False`), then turn 0.9 mm above the seat
  (sag-corrected), 3 deg steps, and 1.5 deg steps each held one step between 45 % and 85 % of the turn (the snap
  zone). 44 steps for -60 deg.

### Mode B in the twin: the key moves in the taker's pads, unseen

G3 mode B on b10 with a perceived in-hand pose (`--held-pose noise`, GT plus noise): **5/14** (100000/03/06/14/23).
With the kinematic belief instead (`--held-belief`, the runtime's attachment; 2 mm key offset; `--belief-drop
fixtures`), no variant inserted a key (b18-b23 on 100000-08, lock yaw exact or believed 10 deg off). Twin traces
(`g3 --trace`, `scratch/pegs_tr2.py`) show why:
- **The taker's grasp is a hinge.** Across the bow's faces, the closing axis (y_key) is a hinge (PhysX has ~no
  torsional friction at the pads). The joint-space move that turns the lying key up for the lock swung it 7.5-8 deg
  about that axis, so its tip sat 5-10 mm off the hole. Turning it up in place about its own x axis keeps x_key
  horizontal, so gravity has no lever about the hinge: 0.1-0.7 deg after it (`regrasp._upright_moves`, mode B
  only). The eased Cartesian carry to the hover (0.04 rad/step) still swings it 3.5 deg (0.07 un-eased: 5).
- **A key on the lock's top does not stop the arm.** Its tooth on the top, the key slides up in the pads and pivots
  (16 mm / 46 deg over 10 steps, 100004 b18). The hand lags its command 0.9-1.1 mm on a 3 mm step and 0.5-0.7 after a
  held step (free: 0.2-0.3 / 0.0). `GUARD_LAG` (4 mm) never fired: "descent reached", let go, the key fell on the top.
  The blind descent now reads the vertical lag over its hover value in 1.5 mm steps through the zone where the bit
  meets the top (contact > 0.5 mm). That fires on the first touch.
- **The ±15 deg sweep is not reliable.** Every touch slides the key up and tilts it further (3.8 -> 20 deg over ten
  probes). Probes at a fixed depth then cleared the top and read as "in" (b19-b22). Re-measuring the contact per probe
  (b23) still ends with the tip beside the hole, because the tip's xy at the hover was 7-10 mm off: the 2 mm belief
  offset, the residual swing, and drift in the giver's pinch of a key believed 2 mm off.
- **Conclusion.** Mode B needs the key's pose in the hand at the hover, the way mode A's servo tail measures it. The
  held-pose read (`2026-10-26-held-pose.md`) never measured the key (the skill was parked); it is the next step,
  together with the lock's yaw from the wrist look. The skill-side pieces stay as defence in depth: the upright turn,
  the look, the vertical-lag descent, and the sweep.

## 6. The belief and handovers (PR #101)

**The gap.** `KinematicBelief` could not represent a handover:
- the taker's close was ignored, because the giver held the object;
- the giver's open dropped it to the table while the taker held it.

That is fatal in mode B for the key, the coin handover and in-air relays. `heldsim` (G3's mode-B emulation) had the
same gap.

**The fix (#101).**
- A closing hand takes a free object at its pads first, else the one the other hand holds (both hold it).
- An open drops only when no other hand still holds the object.
- `heldsim` mirrors both rules.
- Unit tests cover the handover and a plain release. The #84 narrow-close and #93 fixture tests pass unchanged.
