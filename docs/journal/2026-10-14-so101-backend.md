# 2026-10-14 — a real arm: SO-101 with a wrist camera

Owner: "lerobot arm + vacuum cleaner; the test stand is one arm with a cam on the gripper"
(the vacuum base later). Sources compared before choosing (research agents, no side effects):
Gemini 4 Argon (cloud reasoning model, text out; its blog drives an SO-101 by writing code against
an HTTP motion API — a model to plug in, not a harness), Robocurve's inspect-robots (eval harness
with an SO-101 adapter, `lerobot>=0.5`, py3.12 — later, for comparing a trained policy with LLM
control), Waddle Labs' waddle-sdk (supervision layer of their hosted service; SO-101 only in sim —
skipped). Decision: a controlr backend, so every existing experiment axis applies to real hardware.

The rig (`docs/ENVIRONMENT.md`): a laptop with the follower on a CH340 port and a 1080p UVC camera on
the gripper; LeRobot 0.4.2 in conda (py3.10); no NVIDIA GPU; it cannot reach the LLM router, so
controlr runs elsewhere and reaches a localhost-only server through two ssh tunnels (~45 ms RTT,
`observe` with a 1280x720 JPEG ~0.2 s).

Kinematics: TheRobotStudio's `so101_new_calib.urdf` — its joint zero is the middle of the calibrated
range, which is exactly LeRobot's `DEGREES` reading (`(raw - mid) * 360 / 4095`); the rig's resting
pose read [-6, -99, 96, 42, -91] deg, a folded arm, as FK says. 5 joints: straight down the TCP
reaches only z <= ~100 mm (wrist flex limit 95 deg) and x 100..300 mm; tilted 60 deg it covers
x 140..380, z 0..150 — the default, with the tool always pointing away from the base
(`reachable_rotation`). The stock wrist-camera mount (`so101_new_calib_camera.urdf`) sits beside the
jaw-pivot axis; with wrist roll at -90 deg its image is upright (the rig rests there, and the first
frame showed both jaws side by side at the bottom — consistent), hence `tool_roll_deg: 90`.

The manual: `system_v0` says "the axis directions above are the same in every image" — false for a
camera on the gripper. `system_v3` (required with so101) drops the fixed directions; each turn a VIEW
line (computed from FK + camera pose) says which way +x/+y run from the jaw tips in that image; z
is "toward the camera" when the view is within 35 deg of vertical (its image direction there is a
perspective artifact, e.g. "+z points down-left").

Safety in the server, below the model: calibrated-range clamp, joint speed cap, Goal=Present before
torque on (LeRobot's own `connect()` enables torque without it), freeze on a blocked joint (tracking
error), Ctrl-C freeze / double Ctrl-C torque off. The first real check (no motion): the client read
state + a fresh frame through the tunnels; the frame showed the rig laptop's keyboard right under
the jaws — the workspace must be cleared before the first move.

Not done yet: the first motion (needs the owner at the power switch), hand-eye calibration, current
thresholds, a live LLM episode. Unit tests: `tests/test_so101.py` (URDF FK, IK round trip, the
orientation rule, client ↔ real server code with a fake arm over a real connection, blocked joint,
whole FakeLLM episode).

## First motion and first live episode (same evening)

The owner kept the rig laptop in front of the arm, so the work area is above it
(`configs/so101_desk.yaml`). The back-projection of the rest frame with the uncalibrated camera put the
laptop ~8 cm too close (keyboard pitch read 15.8 mm instead of 19 — scale off ~17 %); the home frame
(camera straight down) showed its front edge ~4 cm ahead of the jaws.

Jog (`scripts/so101_jog.py`, ±20 mm per axis, 0.03 m/s): every move ended 5-13 mm LOW and a few mm short
— LeRobot's P=16 leaves the loaded shoulder/elbow short of the goal; home landed at (233, -1, 88)
instead of (240, 0, 100). Integral settle on the server (goal += 0.8 x error until < 0.4 deg, capped
8 deg, the offset carried into the next move) → home (241, 0, 101), every jog within ~1 mm
(e.g. x+ 20.1, z- 20.2 mm). Gripper 1 / 69 mm. VIEW directions match the image (+y move: the scene
shifts right; back: up). Found on the way: restarting the server while the arm held itself up would
have dropped it (motor configuration needs torque off) — the server now refuses to start with torque on.

First live episode (`runs/20261014T214302Z_so101_sticker_reach`, Sonnet 5.5 effort low, no contact):
"centre the open gripper ~30 mm above the blue Intel vPro sticker". 6 turns, LLM 3.2-4.0 s/turn,
cache 0.76, no WARN/STOP; it descended 103 -> 50 mm (deck ~18 mm) with the sticker at the jaw tip and
said DONE. Operator verdict: partial — only ONE jaw shows in the image, so "between the jaws" is not
observable; Sonnet wrote pixel reasoning as prose before its commands (as on RoboDojo). Spend: 6 calls.

## Picking the ball (owner: "pick a ball", gripper never fully open, simple collision detection)

A ~50 mm jute ball on the table against the laptop's front edge. Runs (Sonnet 5.5 effort low):
1. `so101_ball` (tool 60 deg, 75 % open): 25 turns, max_turns — both closes landed IN FRONT of the ball,
   and my x <= 250 mm cap (from the uncalibrated camera, ~17 % scale error) blocked the model's correct
   forward move. 2. Same, x <= 270, 40 turns: 8 closes, all in front — a 60-deg tool cannot straddle a
   ball lying on the table (the sloped jaws meet its top before the tips pass its middle).
3. `so101_ball_down` (tool straight down): two STOPs in 5 turns — 8 deg tracking with the arm stretched
   near the edge of its straight-down reach; owner: no collision, the loaded joints just lagged.
   Collision detection is now current-based (400-600 mA per joint, free moves <= 280 mA) with tracking
   20 deg only as a backstop; the sag offset survives a stop.
4. The fixed jaw rides with the camera; the moving jaw swings out of the image beyond ~40 % (sweep:
   30 % clearly both, 40 % at the edge, 50 % gone) — owner: both jaws must be visible → GRIP open = 40 %.
   Then `so101_ball_down` with "centre the ball between the two jaw tips": 27 turns, 3 closes, the third
   held (jaws stopped at the ball: `holding` true), lifted 30 mm, DONE. Verdict: success — but a human
   hand shows behind the ball just before the grasp (to confirm whether it helped).
The gripper's mm readout is a guessed linear map (it read "18 mm" holding a ~50 mm ball).
